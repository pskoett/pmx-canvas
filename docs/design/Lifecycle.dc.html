Lifecycle and review on cards, boards and the brief

A card inherits its lifecycle from its board’s category and may override it; it never changes its category. Review is a disposition someone records with evidence, not an age-driven state. Four clocks stay separate: reading never counts as validating.

changed The agent’s copy is out of date Changed since the agent read it (wave 1). About delivery, not truth. Clears when the agent reads it again.
review due Is it still valid? About the content. Clears only when a person or agent records an outcome with evidence. Reading or editing never clears it.
Both are amber because both need a look. They differ in glyph, word and fill: a filled chip is the agent’s copy, an outlined chip is the claim. A card can carry both, as Churn by month does: changed sits in the header with the agent’s read marks, review due as a bottom line with a short reason. Decided 2026-10-07 (variant A): that line is the only lifecycle mark a card ever shows, and only while a review is due. A healthy card shows none; rate, inheritance, clocks and history live in ⋯ → Lifecycle; “unknown” shows only on Home folders. A retired card is dimmed in place.
